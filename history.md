# History of Sorry!

## Ancient roots: Pachisi

Sorry! belongs to the "cross and circle" family of race games that descends from **Pachisi**, a game played in India for centuries. In Pachisi, players move pieces around a cross-shaped board according to throws of cowrie shells, and pieces can be captured and sent back to the start.

## Western versions: Ludo and Parcheesi

In the late 1800s, Pachisi was adapted for Western markets:

- **Ludo** was patented in England in 1896 and used a single die.
- **Parcheesi** became popular in the United States, also using dice.

Both kept the core idea: race your pieces home while sending opponents back.

## The invention of Sorry!

Sorry! was patented in England in **1929** by **William Henry Storey** of Southend-on-Sea. His key change was replacing dice with a **deck of cards**, which allowed special moves such as moving backward, splitting a move between two pawns, swapping places, and the famous "Sorry!" bump.

## Publishers

- **Waddingtons** published Sorry! in the United Kingdom in the 1930s.
- **Parker Brothers** acquired the rights and began selling it in the United States in **1934**.
- Parker Brothers was bought by Tonka in 1987, and Tonka was acquired by **Hasbro** in 1991. Hasbro has published Sorry! ever since.

## Later changes

Over the decades the game has been redesigned many times: new artwork, plastic pawns replacing wooden ones, themed and collector's editions, and rule tweaks in some modern editions (see [editions.md](editions.md) and [variants.md](variants.md)). The classic rules, however, have stayed largely the same for nearly a century.
